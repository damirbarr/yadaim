## 2. Legal framework

- **Main rule:** Population and Immigration Authority (רשות האוכלוסין) procedure **9.4.0001**, "Conditions of permit to employ foreign workers in construction by manpower contractors". The corporation is the legal employer. Contractors can't import workers themselves. https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/policy/manpower_construction_foreign_workers_procedure/he/9.4.0001.pdf
- **Contractor eligibility:** Under section 6.1 (per law-firm summaries), workers can be placed **only with contractors that hold a valid רשם הקבלנים registration**, and only for work that fits the contractor's classification. **The product must check the contractor's registration and classification at booking.** https://ktalegal.com/ (articles on 9.4.0001) ; https://www.taagid.co.il/ (summary of 9.4.0001). Registration is mandatory above ~90,472 ILS (main trade) or ~47,256 ILS (sub-trade) per job, per Green Invoice, May 2025. https://www.greeninvoice.co.il/magazine/קבלן-רשום/
- **Permitted trades:** Historically formwork (טפסנות), rebar (ברזלנות), plaster (טייחות) and tiling (ריצוף), on construction sites only. Expansions after 2023 are possible but I could not confirm them **[UNVERIFIED]**. Drywall (גבס) and general skeleton labour may not be eligible trades, so check before listing them.
- **Minimum hours:** Kol Zchut (page ~600 days old) says the construction permit **requires employing the worker at least 211 hours a month, 29 of them paid as overtime** (+50%). This matches agencies selling monthly packages with minimum hours, and it means **the agency carries the cost of idle workers** (strong pain point). https://www.kolzchut.org.il/he/יום_עבודה,_שבוע_עבודה_ושעות_נוספות_לעובדי_בניין_ושיפוצים
- **Placing one worker with different contractors on different days:** **No source confirms or forbids it.** The procedure appears to require the corporation to report each worker's place of work to the Authority, but I could not confirm the reporting window or a minimum placement period **[UNVERIFIED]**. **Get a legal opinion on 9.4.0001 before building per-day switching.** Fallback: minimum booking blocks (e.g., one week) plus "reporting-ready" exports. Moving a worker between corporations is governed by the 2006 regulations (תקנות... מעבר של עובד זר בין מעבידים שהם קבלני כוח אדם בענף הבנין), which corporations are petitioning against. https://www.nevo.co.il/law_html/law00/74050.htm
- **Worker rights and costs (2026):**
  - Minimum wage from 1.4.2026: 6,443.85 ILS/month, 35.40 ILS/hour. Construction wage grades 3-4: 37.91 and 41.76 ILS/hour. https://www.kolzchut.org.il/he/שכר_מינימום_לעובדי_בניין_(שכר_תעריפי)
  - Deposit fund (פיקדון): 13.1% of salary in construction. Withdrawn on exit, minus 15% tax. https://www.bizportal.co.il/career/news/article/20036198
  - Private health insurance: ~150-300 ILS/month. Proper housing is mandatory: ~1,500-3,000 ILS/month (commercial guides).
  - The employer may not charge the worker any fee or broker payment.
  - Levy (היטל עובדים זרים): ~15% of salary for construction corporations, under a High Court challenge.
  - Annual permit fees (אגרות): one guide says ~9,550 ILS/year. Corporations say total government payments reach ~23-24k ILS per worker per year (petition by "דף חדש"). In 2025 the Authority initially refused to renew almost all corporation licenses over a fee dispute. https://www.calcalist.co.il/real-estate/article/r1sawdghwx ; https://www.bizportal.co.il/takdin/news/article/20035616 ; https://www.ice.co.il/realestate/news/article/1046078 ; https://halishka.co.il/ (2026 cost guides)
- **Reforms found:**
  - Renovation sub-sector quota: 5k, but only 844 workers entered by the time of a Knesset discussion. A 2025 incentive encourages corporations to supply renovators.
  - Higher overall cap (Aug 2026), private-track freeze (Aug 2026), India and Sri Lanka going G2G-only (2027), plan to expand foreign executing companies.
  - **No "hourly worker" or day-placement reform found [UNVERIFIED].**

## 3. Pricing

- **No published hourly rates by trade.** Corporations quote privately; agency guides say "no single rate".
- Full monthly cost to the contractor: **13,000-17,000 ILS** (one guide), 8,000-12,000 (another), 7,000-10,000 (a third, probably wage-only). Divided by 211 hours, that is **~62-81 ILS/hour**, or **~560-810 ILS for a 9-hour day**. The claimed 13-17k/month and 565-740/day look consistent. https://www.phantom-mp.co.il/blog/how-much-does-foreign-worker-cost ; https://on-bone.com/כמה-עולה-עובד-זר-לבניין/ ; https://aa-airport.com/ (2026 cost guide) ; https://halishka.co.il/employment-cost-calculator
- Bank of Israel: permit-holding foreign workers cost ~40% less than Israelis. https://boi.org.il/publications/pressreleases/עלות-העסקתם-של-עובדים-זרים-בהיתר-בענפי-הבנייה-והחקלאות-נמוכה-בכ-40-מזו-של-ישראלים/
- Price differences by trade: **[UNVERIFIED]**. My assumption is that experienced formwork, rebar and tiling workers price at the top of the range, and less-experienced or plaster workers lower. Validate with 5-10 agency quotes.

## 4. Competitors

  - Has a directory of corporations (profile, contacts, permits) and matching by experience, language and trade.
  - Claims it lists only 9.4.0001-compliant corporations, and publishes heavy SEO content.
  - **No evidence of per-day booking, worker-level profiles, check-in, hours approval or transparent pricing.** It is a lead-gen and directory model. Its pricing model was not found.

## 5. UX insights

- No Israel-specific survey on digital adoption or WhatsApp use in construction was found **[UNVERIFIED]**. The product context (mobile-first, WhatsApp-heavy, Hebrew, Excel in back offices) is plausible but unconfirmed.
- Design implications:
  - WhatsApp deep links or notifications for booking confirmations.
  - Hebrew RTL, mobile web/PWA for site managers.
  - Excel import and export for agencies.
  - Reporting-ready exports for Authority compliance.
- Workers: the Histadrut gives safety training with interpreters, and workers are vulnerable because of language barriers. Worker-facing screens (schedule, site address, check-in) should support Hindi, Sinhala, Romanian/Russian, Uzbek, Mandarin and Thai, with icons and voice.
- Skill mismatch is a top contractor complaint (the Maariv "shoemakers and gardeners" quote). Verified trade, experience and ratings, plus "no-fit = no pay", address it directly.

## 6. Recommendations (my analysis)

- **Pilot trades:** **Formwork (טפסנות)** for volume, plus **tiling (ריצוף)** for short, task-based demand from finishing and renovation contractors, who suffer most and are most open to flexible booking. Both are core permitted trades. Avoid drywall (גבס) until its eligibility is confirmed.
- **Pilot region:** Center and Gush Dan, plus Sharon. This is where building starts and lodging are densest, and short distances make per-day moves practical. Jerusalem is the second choice. **[Regional starts not re-verified this round.]**
- **Differentiators:**
  1. Worker-level booking with **all-inclusive daily price**, unlike Taagidnet's lead-gen.
  2. **Compliance engine:** contractor רשם הקבלנים and classification check, worker visa and certificate expiry, Authority reporting export.
  3. **Idle-worker monetisation** for agencies, which must pay 211 hours a month anyway.
  4. Check-in, hours approval and ratings that build trust about skill.
  5. Multilingual worker app.
- **Pricing (suggested, to validate):**
  - **Subscription:** 20-35 ILS per active worker per month, with a ~500 ILS/month minimum per agency. Example: 200 workers × 25 = 5,000 ILS/month. That is under 0.2% of worker cost.
  - **Commission:** 2.5-4 ILS per approved hour (~4-5% of a 62-81 ILS/hour rate) on marketplace-originated bookings only. That is ~22-36 ILS per 9-hour day. Charge no commission, or ~1 ILS/hour, on the agency's existing contracts managed in the tool.
  - Bill monthly with invoice export.
- **Key risks:**
  - Legality of per-day switching under 9.4.0001: get legal counsel.
  - Quota freeze and the 2027 India and Sri Lanka change, which constrain supply but increase the value of using idle workers.
  - Fee disputes squeezing agency margins, so they will be price-sensitive.
